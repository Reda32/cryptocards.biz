## Who is the Kosh card for?

Kosh is built for people who earn in dollars but live outside the United States — freelancers on Upwork, Fiverr, Toptal and Deel, creators paid through YouTube, and remote workers in Nigeria, Pakistan, India, the Philippines, Egypt, Kenya and similar markets. It solves a specific, painful problem: getting paid in USD and then being able to spend it without converting to a local currency that may lose value.

It is not for US residents, who are generally excluded, and it is not the cheapest option if you spend mostly in currencies other than USD. If you want a plain, low-fee dollar card with no account layer, a simpler product will suit you better.

## How the card works

You open a Kosh account and get named USD account details with ACH and wire routing, plus SWIFT details in supported regions. You add those details to the platforms that pay you, and the money arrives into your Kosh account where it is held as USDC or USDT. When you spend with the card, the transaction draws from that stablecoin balance directly — there is no separate top-up step and no need to move money to a bank first.

The result is that your earnings stay in dollars end to end, and you can spend at Visa merchants online, in person, or through Apple Pay and Google Pay.

## Fees, rewards and limits

Account creation is free and USD card spending carries a 0% platform fee, which is the card's strongest selling point. Everything else carries a cost: a virtual card of around US$10, a physical card of roughly US$60–100, a 1.5–2% FX fee plus a small flat charge on non-USD spending, and ATM withdrawals at about US$1 plus 1%.

Rewards are 5% cashback on hotel bookings and KOSH Points on eligible spending, with category multipliers and redemption for gift cards, hotel discounts or lounge access. Points are in-app credit rather than cash you can withdraw, and the rates are set by Kosh and can change — so treat the headline percentages as promotional, not contractual.

## KYC, availability and risk

Verification requires an identity document and may ask for supporting documents such as a bank statement. Users report that onboarding can be confusing, that country eligibility has shifted over time — Hong Kong for individuals being one example — and that sign-up sometimes routes through a third party. Availability is advertised at 90+ countries but is country-specific, and the US is generally unsupported.

The main risk to understand is custody. Balances are held as stablecoins in smart-contract wallets with partner custody for card settlement, not as bank deposits, so there is no FDIC-style protection. Kosh is operated by the CopperX team with named backers and regulated card partners, and it has passed third-party domain safety scans, but independent reviews are still scarce and dispute handling is not well documented. Use it as a spending account, not a place to park large balances.

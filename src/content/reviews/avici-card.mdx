## Who is the Avici card for?

Avici suits crypto users who do not want to pre-load a card. Instead of moving money onto the card, you keep USDC in your own wallet as collateral and spend against it, like a secured credit card. With no transaction fee and no FX markup from Avici, it is a cheap way to spend if you live in one of its supported countries.

It is not for you if you want cashback: Avici pays no rewards on purchases. It is also unavailable in many large markets, including China, India, Russia, Turkey, Vietnam and Israel, and in the US it covers only 31 states.

## How the card works

Avici is a secured credit card. Your USDC stays in your wallet, and Visa gives you a credit line with matching spending power. Visa can only take funds when there is a proven card spend. You can top up with any token: Avici swaps it to USDC automatically, and Solana deposits are bridged in the background.

All your cards, virtual and physical, share one spending balance, and you can set an individual limit for each card. Avici does not set a fixed spending limit, but its terms say the limit is based on your collateral and can be changed at any time, including cut to zero. The card works with Apple Pay and Google Pay at no extra cost.

## Cards and fees

- **Platinum (virtual):** US$15 one-off for your first card, US$10 for each extra one. A basic card with no special benefits.
- **Platinum (physical):** US$50 one-off.
- **Signature (virtual):** US$30 for the first year, then US$20 a year.
- **Signature (physical):** US$75, then US$20 a year from the second year.

Signature adds Visa Signature benefits such as airport lounge access (you pay for visits), auto rental insurance, purchase protection, luxury hotel access and emergency medical cover abroad.

Running costs are low:

- **Transactions and FX:** no transaction fee and no FX markup from Avici. Visa may add a 0.4% to 1% cross-border fee on non-USD spending.
- **ATM:** US$1 + 0.65% per withdrawal on Platinum, free on Signature. Up to three withdrawals a day, US$1,000 per withdrawal and US$3,000 a day. One Avici page quotes a lower US$250 daily figure, so check the app.

The card fee is charged when you order, **before** identity verification. If you fail verification, you have to ask support for a refund, so check that your country and state are supported first.

## KYC and availability

Verification runs through Sumsub. You choose your account type and country of residence, enter your details, upload an ID document and complete a liveness check. Common reasons for rejection include details that do not match your documents, regional restrictions, and domestic IDs that Sumsub does not recognise.

Avici lists about 50 supported countries, including Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Hong Kong, Japan, Mexico, Singapore, South Africa and the UAE, plus 31 US states. Residents of Belarus, China, Cuba, India, Iran, Iraq, Israel, Myanmar, Nepal, Nicaragua, North Korea, Russia, Sri Lanka, Syria, Turkey, Ukraine, Venezuela and Vietnam cannot use it.

## Verdict

Avici is a good choice if you live in a supported country and want to spend crypto without pre-loading a card or paying FX markups. The virtual Platinum card at US$15 is the cheapest way in; Signature is worth it mainly for free ATM withdrawals and Visa Signature protections. If cashback matters to you, look elsewhere.

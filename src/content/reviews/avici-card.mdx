## Who is the Avici card for?

Avici targets people who are already native to on-chain finance. If your balance lives in a Solana wallet and you are uncomfortable moving it into a custodial account just to spend it, Avici's self-custody model is the pitch: the funds stay in your wallet until the moment of purchase.

It is a weaker fit if you want boring, predictable costs or if you are new to crypto. The collateral mechanics and an evolving fee schedule mean you need to understand what you are signing up for.

## How the card works

Avici issues a Visa Signature card that you fund from your own wallet. Because it is built on Solana, transactions are gas-sponsored on Solana and Base, so you are not paying network fees on every top-up. You can add the virtual card to Apple Pay or Google Pay and pay contactlessly while your crypto stays put.

The collateral-based credit model is the differentiator. Instead of selling your holdings to load the card, you borrow against them, which keeps your exposure intact but introduces liquidation risk if the market moves sharply against you.

## Fees, rewards and limits

There is no monthly fee, no issuance fee and no top-up fee, and limits are advertised as unlimited. Rewards are a headline 5% cashback paid in crypto, which is high for the category. The costs hide in the details: around 1% FX on non-USD spending that can climb to 3%, and ATM withdrawals at US$1 plus 0.65%. Read the current schedule before relying on the cashback.

## KYC and availability

Avici requires identity verification before issuing a card, but stops short of proof of address or a selfie at this tier. It is not available in India, China, Russia or sanctioned countries. As a newer platform, both the fee structure and regional availability are still settling, so treat the numbers as a snapshot and re-check before you apply.

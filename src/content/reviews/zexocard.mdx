## Who is ZexoCard for?

ZexoCard suits people who want a virtual card funded with crypto without going through a full identity check. Standard sign-up needs only an email, there is no monthly fee, and the Everyday card works with Apple Pay and Google Pay. It is also one of the few cards with a dedicated option for paying AI tools, ad platforms and cloud services.

It is a poor fit if you make lots of small purchases, because every Everyday card payment costs US$0.25. It also leaves some details unpublished: ZexoCard does not state its funding fee rate or FX fee, and whether you get Visa or Mastercard depends on the card product.

## How the card works

You sign up on zexocard.com with your email and fund your ZexoCard wallet with crypto. The site lists Bitcoin, USDT, USDC, Ethereum and Solana as funding options. You then issue a card for a one-off US$10 fee and top it up from your wallet whenever you like; the card is reloadable.

ZexoCard offers two kinds of virtual card:

- **Everyday:** for travel bookings, online shopping and contactless spending. You can add it to Apple Pay or Google Pay on eligible devices. Each purchase costs US$0.25, deducted from the card balance.
- **Subscription:** for supported AI, advertising and cloud merchants, with no per-purchase merchant fee.

Each card allows up to US$100,000 per transaction and US$500,000 per calendar month, with no separate daily cap. The card balance must cover the payment plus any fees. To reveal your full card details, you need your account PIN.

## Fees in practice

- **Card issuance:** US$10 one-off per card.
- **Account, monthly maintenance and balance checks:** free.
- **Everyday purchases:** US$0.25 each. On a US$10 purchase that is 2.5%; on a US$200 purchase it is about 0.1%.
- **Subscription card purchases:** no merchant transaction fee.
- **Funding:** a funding fee, plus any network charges for sending crypto, is shown in your quote before you pay. ZexoCard does not publish the rate, but it has a fee calculator on its site.
- **FX:** not published. Check before using the card in other currencies.

Because the per-purchase fee is flat, the Everyday card works out cheapest for fewer, larger payments. For recurring AI or cloud bills, the Subscription card avoids the fee altogether.

## KYC and availability

ZexoCard says standard onboarding requires no routine identity-document submission: you sign up with an email, and you do not need a bank account because you fund your wallet with crypto.

Availability works by exclusion. Accounts cannot be opened, and cards cannot be issued or loaded, from **North Korea, Iran, Syria, Cuba, Belarus, Afghanistan or Myanmar**, or from the **Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk** regions of Ukraine. Everywhere else you can register, although card ordering and funding depend on those services being enabled in your account. Do not use false location details to get around the list; ZexoCard says it may change as partner requirements change.

Where you spend is a separate question. The card works at merchants that accept its network, Visa or Mastercard as shown on your card, and its product type. A Subscription card, for example, is meant for subscription and advertising merchants, so a Visa or Mastercard logo at checkout does not guarantee every card product will be accepted. Balances are held in USD, and conversion charges on purchases in other currencies depend on the card product.

## Verdict

ZexoCard is a practical choice if you want a crypto-funded virtual card without an ID check, especially for online subscriptions, where the Subscription card skips the per-purchase fee. The US$10 issuance fee is reasonable and the limits are high. The trade-offs are the US$0.25 fee on every Everyday purchase and the unpublished funding and FX fees, so run a small test top-up first.

## Who is the Nexo card for?

The Nexo card is aimed at long-term crypto holders who would rather borrow against their assets than sell them. Its dual-mode design is the reason to consider it: in Debit Mode your balance keeps earning interest until the instant you spend, and in Credit Mode you draw on a collateralised credit line instead of touching your holdings.

It suits experienced users in the EEA and UK who are comfortable holding NEXO tokens to reach the best tiers. It is a poor fit for US residents, for anyone who wants a physical card right now, or for people looking for a simple no-strings spending card.

## How the card works

You fund a Nexo account, choose Debit or Credit Mode, and spend through a Mastercard. In Debit Mode, idle balances can earn up to 14% APY until the moment of a transaction — a genuinely unusual feature. In Credit Mode, purchases are funded by a loan backed by your crypto, which preserves your exposure but adds interest and liquidation risk if collateral values fall.

Rewards are paid in NEXO or BTC at up to 2% for top-tier users, credited daily with no lock-up. The catch, documented extensively by the community, is that the effective rate is lower than the headline once internal conversion spreads are accounted for, and UK residents cannot earn crypto cashback at all under FCA rules.

## Fees and limits

There is no monthly or annual fee, FX is cheap at around 0.2% on weekdays in the EEA, and ATM withdrawals are free up to €2,000 per month. Costs appear in the edges: a weekend FX surcharge, charges above the ATM allowance, and credit-mode interest that is materially higher outside the upper loyalty tiers.

## KYC and availability

Nexo runs full KYC through automated verification: an identity document, proof of address and a liveness check. It is limited to the EEA and UK, is not available in the US, and new physical card orders have been paused since early 2025 — so most new users start, and may stay, on the virtual card.

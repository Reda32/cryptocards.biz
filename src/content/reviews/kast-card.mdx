## Who is the KAST card for?

KAST is built around the idea that you should be able to spend a stablecoin balance quickly, without a long onboarding process. The email-only sign-up and instant virtual card make it attractive if you want to test a crypto card before committing documents, or if you move between countries and value Apple Pay over a physical card.

It is less appealing if you already hold a wide mix of coins, or if you need high limits from day one. KAST is deliberately stablecoin-first, and the ceiling stays low until you verify.

## How the card works

You sign up with an email, get a virtual Visa card, and fund it with stablecoins. Spending settles in dollars, so cross-currency purchases are straightforward and the FX markup is competitive. Rewards are paid in stablecoins rather than points, which keeps the value transparent.

The physical metal card is an upgrade: it costs a one-off fee and a monthly fee, and it is the gateway to higher limits and ATM access. For most people the free virtual tier is enough, especially combined with a phone wallet.

## Fees and limits in practice

The virtual tier is genuinely free to hold. The costs appear when you cash out at an ATM or upgrade to metal. The FX fee of around 1% is among the lower end for crypto cards, and stablecoin top-ups do not carry a conversion spread.

The catch is the pre-KYC limit. It is fine for daily spending but too low for large purchases, so decide early whether you want to verify.

## KYC and compliance

KAST's selling point is the "basic" tier: no ID to start. That is enough for a capped virtual card. Raising limits or ordering the physical card pulls you into full KYC with a government ID, at which point the experience looks much like any other regulated card.

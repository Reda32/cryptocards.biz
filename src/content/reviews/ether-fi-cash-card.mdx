## Who is the Ether.fi Cash card for?

Ether.fi Cash is aimed at people who hold ETH or a liquid staking token and do not want to sell it to fund everyday spending. Instead of loading a card with cash, you collateralise a self-custodied vault and borrow against it, so your position stays intact and keeps earning yield.

It is a poor fit for crypto beginners or anyone who wants quick, low-friction onboarding. The mechanics (collateral ratios, borrow mode, liquidation thresholds) take some learning, and the compliance checks are the single most common source of frustration in user reports.

## How the card works

You deposit collateral, borrow USDC against it, and spend that USDC through a Visa card. Transactions settle on Scroll, a Layer 2, with gasless execution, which keeps the experience close to a normal card. Apple Pay and Google Pay are supported.

Because you are borrowing rather than selling, there is no taxable disposal of your crypto, and your collateral continues to accrue staking yield while it is pledged. The trade-off is that the loan is over-collateralised: if the value of your collateral drops sharply, it can be liquidated unless you top up or repay.

## Fees, rewards and limits

The card has no monthly fee and generous limits: US$30,000 per day and unlimited monthly spend. Costs sit in the details: a US$40 issuance fee, around 1% FX outside USD/EUR, a 2% ATM fee capped at US$250 per day, and interest once promotional 0% periods end.

Cashback is advertised at up to 3% with no category caps, paid in WETH or SCR. Read the bands carefully: the rate steps down as monthly spending rises, so heavy users will not earn the headline rate across their whole spend. Rewards paid in volatile tokens are also worth less if the price falls before you sell.

## KYC, availability and support

Ether.fi asks for an email, phone number and a government ID, but not proof of address or a selfie at this tier. In practice, users report long address-verification waits and jurisdictional blocks, including across a reported twenty US states. It is also unavailable in India, Indonesia and the Netherlands.

Support is the other weak spot in community reports: account freezes are described as opaque and slow to resolve. If you are comfortable with DeFi mechanics and value non-custodial spending, the product itself is well reviewed; if you want predictable, human support, temper your expectations.

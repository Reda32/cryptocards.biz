## Who is the Rizon Card for?

Rizon is built for people who want a US dollar account funded with stablecoins and a Visa card to spend it, especially freelancers and remote workers in the US and Latin America who get paid in USDC or USDT. If you mostly spend in US dollars, the card is cheap: USD card spending carries no fee and the virtual card costs US$10.

It is a weaker choice if you spend mainly in other currencies, where every purchase costs 1.7% + US$0.10, or if you want cashback, which Rizon does not offer. And if you live in the EU, Rizon says it does not actively offer its services there.

## How the card works

You sign up with an email, complete a short in-app KYC check, and fund a USD balance with USDC or USDT. You then spend through a Visa Platinum card that works online, in stores and with Apple Pay and Google Pay. The card is issued through Rain. Rizon's fee table lists no limit on card spending or deposits, and the card supports ATM cash withdrawals.

Rizon also offers transfers and payouts, such as receiving money from platforms like Deel, which is the main reason to choose it over a pure spending card.

## Fees and plans

- **Virtual card:** US$10 standard price. Rizon regularly runs offers that cut this sharply, so check the app before ordering.
- **Account maintenance:** free.
- **USD card spending:** free.
- **Non-USD card spending:** 1.7% + US$0.10 per transaction on the standard plan.
- **Emerald plan:** from US$5.59 a month. It halves FX, trading and transaction fees, and Rizon's comparison pages say it includes a free physical card.
- **ATM:** withdrawals are supported, but Rizon does not clearly publish the fee or limit.

A caveat: Rizon's own pages quote different numbers. One page shows a 1.5% FX fee instead of 1.7%, and Emerald is priced at US$6.99 a month on another, and one page lists the physical card as "coming soon". We use the main Fees and Limits page, but confirm the current figures in the app.

Emerald only pays for itself if you spend a lot in other currencies. Halving a 1.7% fee saves 0.85%, so at US$5.59 a month you need roughly US$650 or more of non-USD spending each month to break even.

## KYC and availability

Rizon requires a short in-app KYC check after email sign-up but does not publish the exact documents. It does not publish a supported-country list either: its pages cite 122+ countries in one place and 178 in another, and its terms say it focuses on the US and Latin America. The card terms exclude Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Russia, Belarus, Albania and Ukraine (including Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk). Check the country picker in the app before signing up.

## Verdict

Rizon is a sensible, low-cost card for spending dollar stablecoins in US dollars, with Apple Pay and Google Pay and a US$10 virtual card. It is less competitive for foreign-currency spending and offers no rewards. Its biggest drawback is transparency: fees and coverage vary between its own pages, so verify in the app before you fund the account.
